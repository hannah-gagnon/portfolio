Dream Creatives

Three days to turn a kiosk-first site into a desktop experience people could understand and trust. 1st place project @ FLUI 2026.

Latest update

After the jam, Dream Creatives adopted our designs nearly in full. The new site is live at dreamcreatives.ca.

(01) Overview

The site was built for a kiosk, not the web. At first glance, nobody knew what they were looking at.

Dream Creatives is an AI-powered photo booth reimagining event photography. Our team had three days to redesign the homepage and main user flow.

Our approach

At first glance we were fairly lost, unsure what Dream Creatives was offering. The original kiosk design didn’t feel or look trustworthy, and everyone was lost as to what they actually did. So we did an audit to better understand.

Methods & constraints

Heuristic review, user flow mapping and low-to-high fidelity wireframes, all in a 3-day remote jam with no direct access to users.

(02) Clarify the product

We wanted to make it obvious what Dream Creatives offers & does.

The challenge

Visitors landed with no context, no clear hierarchy and no obvious next step. We needed a landing page that welcomed first-timers and returning clients alike.

What we did

We rebuilt the homepage as a scrollable landing page with clearer navigation, balancing the client’s “luxury” and “corporate” audiences.

(03) Build trust

AI photos of real people? Trust was not optional.

The challenge

Privacy and data questions make visitors hesitate. Without trust, they don’t take the next step. Previously, there was no privacy information on the DC website.

What we did

We added a disclaimer page, a trust bar and a pricing page so people understand the pricing model and how their information is used.

(04) Build community

People want to share their photos. We gave them somewhere to do it.

The challenge

The old site had a way to share, but not without proper permissions & gallery space. We needed a community layer so Dream Creatives felt like somewhere to belong, not a one-off service.

What we did

We revamped the public gallery so visitors can share and browse, and added CTAs and social proof to surface the community on the homepage.

(05) Final thoughts

Zero hackathon experience, one amazing team, and very little sleep.

Reflection

My friend Gia and I joined with no hackathon experience and pretty low expectations. As project manager, figuring out how a new team communicated and kept moving in such a short window was a big part of the challenge. We had the absolute best team members and a fantastic mentor. It was a total blast.
